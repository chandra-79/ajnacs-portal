The retrospective runs. The team fills the board with post-its. The facilitator groups them. The team discusses. The hour ends. The board is photographed. The action items column is empty, or contains items that were never committed to with names and dates.

The next sprint is identical to the last one.

This is not a retrospective process problem. It is a retrospective outcome problem.

## What retrospectives are supposed to produce

A retrospective is an improvement meeting. The output is a specific set of changes to how the team works — changes that have a named owner, a target sprint or date, and enough specificity to know whether they were completed.

If the output is themes, observations, and discussion notes with no committed changes, the retrospective produced a record of problems, not a resolution of them.

## Why retrospectives produce themes instead of changes

**Facilitators conflate discussion with action.** A productive discussion about why the sprint was challenging is useful for shared understanding. It is not a substitute for a committed action. The facilitator who treats a rich discussion as a successful retrospective is optimizing for engagement over outcome.

**"More of" and "less of" items are not actionable.** "More cross-team communication." "Less interruption during focus time." "More documentation." These are desires, not actions. An action is: "Schedule a 30-minute weekly sync between our team and the data team, starting next sprint. [Name] owns the calendar invite." A desire has no owner and no completion criterion.

**Teams avoid committing to changes because changes require time.** A committed action item takes time to complete. That time comes from somewhere. Teams that are already overloaded resist committing to more work, even work that would make them less overloaded. The facilitator needs to help the team explicitly protect time for improvement work.

**Velocity pressure kills follow-through.** Action items from retrospectives compete with feature work for sprint capacity. Feature work has clearer business value and more visible stakeholder pressure. Retrospective actions get cut. The patterns that were identified persist.

## The format that produces outcomes

End every retrospective with a specific section: "What are we committing to change next sprint?" One to three items maximum. Each item has: what, who, when. The facilitator reads these aloud to confirm the team understands the commitments. The next retrospective opens with: what happened to the commitments from last time?

This is uncomfortable because it creates accountability. It is also the only thing that makes retrospectives produce change rather than records.

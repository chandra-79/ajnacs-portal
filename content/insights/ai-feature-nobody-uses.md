AI adoption inside enterprise products fails in predictable ways, and the failure pattern is almost never about the underlying model capability.

The most common failure: the feature was designed around what the AI could do rather than around a specific user task that was taking too long. The team demonstrated the capability in a demo that impressed leadership and then built something that users tried once, got an inconsistent result, and didn't return to.

The second failure: the workflow integration. An AI feature that adds a step — open a panel, enter a prompt, review output, copy to the actual work area — is not a productivity improvement for users who were already doing the task in 30 seconds. The features that get used replace a step that was frustrating, not add a step that is technically impressive.

The third failure: no feedback loop. If users cannot easily indicate when the AI output is wrong, the team cannot improve the model or the retrieval, and users cannot correct the output efficiently. The feature becomes something you use when the stakes are low and ignore when they matter.

Adoption is a user behavior change. Engineering the capability is the smaller part of the problem.

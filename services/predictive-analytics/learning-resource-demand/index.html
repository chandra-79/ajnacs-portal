Forecast demand for optional learning support

Hypothetical aggregate resource forecasting for tutoring capacity, with no automated student eligibility decisions.

#Proposed decision and boundaries

A hypothetical education service wants to plan staffing for optional tutoring sessions. The proposed analytics would forecast aggregate session demand by subject and week. It would not score individual students for admission, funding or discipline, and would not decide who deserves support. All demand values and outcomes here are synthetic illustrations.

Discovery would define booked sessions, attended sessions, cancellations and unmet requests separately. Attendance can be constrained by available capacity, so it may understate demand when sessions are full. The team would identify that censoring and agree on the planning target before selecting a model. Data access would favor aggregate, purpose-appropriate records while checking whether small groups could still reveal individuals.

#Design and implementation

A versioned dataset would combine prior booking activity, known academic-calendar events and published session availability. Future examination changes or later attendance corrections would not enter historical forecasts before they were known. A simple seasonal baseline would be compared with a candidate model on later terms or weeks, with explicit handling of unusual closures.

Implementation would include a synthetic fixture with a holiday, a cancelled session and a fully booked week. The output would show forecast horizon, interval where justified and data-quality status. Planners would retain the decision to add capacity. The NIST AI RMF is useful background for documenting intended use and avoiding expansion into a more consequential student-level decision without review.

#Synthetic capacity calculation

Assume a planning week has a point forecast of eighty sessions and a chosen upper planning estimate of one hundred sessions. Each tutor can provide ten sessions that week. Planning to the point estimate needs 80/10 = eight tutor allocations; planning to the upper estimate needs 100/10 = ten. The chart compares eight and ten allocations.

The two additional allocations provide twenty sessions of capacity, but the example does not establish that they are required or cost-effective. The upper estimate's coverage must be evaluated over representative periods, and the cost of unused capacity should be considered alongside unmet demand. Calling an estimate an upper bound does not make it a guarantee.

#Acceptance and handover

Acceptance would require clear distinction between attendance and demand, temporally valid inputs, useful comparison with the baseline and a tested manual planning fallback. A pilot would track actual requests, available slots, attendance and unmet demand separately. It would also assess whether forecasts shift resources away from small subjects whose needs are poorly represented in aggregate averages.

Rollback would restore the existing planning method and preserve forecasts for later analysis. Handover would include calendar assumptions, aggregation rules, interval interpretation and a rehearsal of an unexpected campus closure. The limitation is that aggregate forecasts cannot explain every student's circumstances; the service should support accessible optional resources without turning a capacity estimate into a judgment about an individual learner.

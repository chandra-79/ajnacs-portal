Hypothetical · Healthcare administration

Prepare appointment administration drafts without clinical decisions

Hypothetical scheduling support with scoped records, explicit uncertainty and staff-controlled communication.

#Proposed problem and boundary

A hypothetical outpatient organization wants to reduce repetitive appointment-administration work. The proposed assistant would summarize scheduling requests, identify missing administrative details and prepare staff-reviewed responses. It would not diagnose symptoms, recommend treatment, determine urgency or change a clinical plan. All cases and measurements here are synthetic; a real deployment would need its own privacy and regulatory review.

Discovery would map the exact information needed for scheduling and remove unnecessary clinical content from model inputs. Staff would identify requests that must leave the administrative workflow, such as a message asking for medical advice. The accepted outcome would be a correct administrative draft or an appropriate escalation, with no unsupported promise about availability or care.

#Proposed design and delivery

A scoped scheduling adapter would return permitted appointment slots and administrative policy references. The model would receive only the relevant request and records. A deterministic service would check slot availability again before any booking proposal is executed. Staff approval would bind the selected patient record, slot and communication destination, while the backend verifies access independently.

The implementation fixture would use fictional people and calendars. Tests would include two people with similar names, a cancelled slot, a missing contact preference and a request containing clinical language. The assistant would stop and route the clinical request to the established staff process. Retrieved text would remain untrusted, consistent with the defensive boundary described by OWASP's prompt-injection guidance.

#Synthetic measurement example

Suppose a rehearsal contains 100 requests: seventy ordinary scheduling requests and thirty needing clarification or escalation. The assistant prepares sixty-three acceptable ordinary drafts and correctly routes twenty-seven exceptional requests. The chart shows seventy ordinary inputs, sixty-three acceptable drafts, thirty exceptional inputs and twenty-seven correct routes. Success is 63/70 = 90% for ordinary drafting and 27/30 = 90% for routing.

The combined 90/100 accepted disposition rate is only an illustration. It would not establish clinical safety, privacy compliance or suitability for production. Reviewers would separately inspect wrong-person access, inappropriate clinical content and unauthorized communication as critical failures rather than averaging them into a quality score.

#Acceptance, fallback and handover

A limited pilot would require a reviewed data boundary, accurate record matching, correct administrative scope and a usable staff review screen. Effort measurement would include time spent checking and correcting drafts. An attractive draft that takes longer to verify than writing from scratch would not justify expansion.

Rollback would disable assistant-generated suggestions while preserving the existing scheduling workflow. Pending drafts would be clearly marked unapproved and would never be sent automatically after recovery. Handover would include escalation ownership, slot-conflict handling, retention rules and an exercise in which staff correct a wrong administrative assumption. The proposed benefit is reduced clerical effort with inspectable boundaries, not replacement of clinical judgment.

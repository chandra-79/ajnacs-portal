Allocate a shared platform without inventing savings

#Hypothetical problem and scope

A fictional B2B software company operates three product groups on a shared data platform. Its monthly report assigns the whole platform bill to the infrastructure team, making product-level comparisons difficult. The proposed engagement would create an explainable showback model for one shared cost pool. It would not claim that assigning costs to products reduces the provider invoice or establishes exact customer profitability.

Discovery would reconcile the selected billing period and identify direct versus shared resources. The team would request read-only billing exports, validated product ownership, and approved consumption metrics. The data contract would define currency, cost basis, period boundaries, late adjustments, and the meaning of the allocation driver. Sensitive customer identifiers would be replaced with controlled internal mappings where possible.

#Proposed allocation design

Directly attributable charges would remain direct. For the shared pool, product owners would review whether measured compute consumption is a reasonable driver for the intended management decision. The policy would document excluded costs, missing measurements, and a residual category. A driver that is convenient but unrelated to consumption would be identified as a policy choice rather than a precise causal model.

Implementation would produce a versioned transformation and a reconciliation report. The allocated amounts plus any residual must equal the selected shared pool. Tests would cover a product with no usage, a late correction, duplicate ingestion, and a missing ownership mapping. The team would preview the results with affected owners before using the figures in budgets or chargeback.

#Synthetic calculation and chart

Assume a reconciled shared pool of 6,000 currency units and validated driver shares of 60%, 30%, and 10% for products A, B, and C. Allocation gives 3,600, 1,800, and 600 respectively. The sum remains 6,000. The chart shows these synthetic allocations, not before-and-after savings. The example assumes the driver shares cover the whole selected pool and does not include taxes, support, or other excluded charges.

Acceptance would require reproducible totals, owner-approved mappings, and a documented explanation of the driver. A sensitivity check could compare the result with an alternative policy, such as reserved capacity, to reveal whether management conclusions depend heavily on the choice. The organization would decide whether that uncertainty is acceptable for showback or whether stronger measurement is needed.

#Handover and governance

The old report would remain available while the new policy is reviewed, and policy changes would be versioned rather than rewriting history silently. Handover would include reconciliation queries, exception ownership, and a monthly mapping review. FinOps allocation guidance informs the approach. The company, cost pool, and shares are hypothetical; no invoice reduction is implied.
